The AI Governance Gap

Tracking where policy ends and behavior breaks down

Organizations are building AI governance frameworks at record pace — but incidents keep rising. Our Behavioral Sufficiency research found that governance correlates with incident visibility, not prevention (r = 0.36). Having rules on paper doesn't change behavior: 63% of documented AI incidents can't even be attributed to a responsible country, and incident volume grew 126% between 2022–2024 despite a surge in new policy frameworks. The problem isn't a lack of governance — it's that governance alone isn't sufficient to change how organizations actually behave.

This map asks the next question: where exactly is behavior breaking down, and how? Each of the 3,209 records below is classified not just by severity, but by the behavioral failure behind it — whether a governance gap left room for harm, a culture deficit let warnings go ignored, reporting failures kept incidents hidden, accountability was absent when things went wrong, or norms eroded over time until failure became routine. Click any bubble to see what's happening in that region.

Critical Rubric score 9–12. Physical harm or safety threat at population scale, irreversible consequences affecting vulnerable groups (children, patients in acute care, military personnel).
High Rubric score 7–8. Financial loss >$10k or rights violations affecting a community, difficult to reverse. Often involves at-risk populations (elderly, low-income, minorities).
Medium Rubric score 5–6. Operational disruption or reputational harm affecting an identifiable group, usually reversible with effort (legal action, policy change).
Low Rubric score 3–4. Minor operational issues or inconvenience affecting individuals, easily corrected with a software fix or process adjustment.
Informational Rubric score 0–2. Policy documents, use-case inventories, and governance frameworks with no direct harm component.
Rubric dimensions: Each record is scored 0–3 on four dimensions — Harm Type (none → physical/safety), Scale (none → population-level), Reversibility (n/a → irreversible), and Vulnerability (n/a → children/life-critical). The four scores are summed (max 12) and mapped deterministically to severity. Critical and high ratings undergo a second review pass to guard against over-classification.

Reading the map: Bubble fill color reflects the weighted average severity across all records for that country. A colored ring appears when ≥15% of records are critical or high — signaling concentrated risk even when the average is diluted by a large volume of lower-severity records.
